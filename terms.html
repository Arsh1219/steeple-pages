What Steeple is

Steeple gives you one Bible verse each day with a short plain English explanation, a reflection question, a widget, a reading streak, and a way to ask a question about the verse you are reading.

Steeple is a reading aid. It is not a church, not a counsellor, and not a substitute for either.

The free version

You can use Steeple without paying and without an account. The daily verse, its explanation, the reflection question, the widget, the daily reminder and the streak are free and stay free. The free version also includes a limited number of questions in Ask and a limited number of saved verses.

Ask, and its limits

Ask uses an AI model to answer a question about the verse you are reading.

  • Answers are generated and can be wrong. Read them as a starting point, not as authority. Check anything that matters against the text itself and against people you trust.
  • Ask answers questions about the verse on screen. It will decline questions about other things.
  • Ask does not give medical, legal, financial or mental health advice, and you should not rely on it for any of those.
  • Ask does not speak for God, does not deliver personal messages, and does not predict the future. If it ever appears to, that is a malfunction, and we would like to know about it.
  • We may limit how many questions can be asked in a day to keep the service running.

Content and beliefs

The explanations aim to be broadly Protestant, non denominational, and fair to the text. Where Christians genuinely read a passage differently, the app tries to say so rather than pick a side. We will get this wrong sometimes. Tell us when we do and we will look at it.

Bible text is used from the World English Bible and the King James Version, both in the public domain.
